Perspective · March 2026

Content models are arguments about meaning

Every content model encodes a claim about what matters and what can be told apart.

A content model looks like plumbing — field types, validation rules, reference cardinality — and it gets handed to whoever is closest to the CMS. But the moment you decide that a case study has a client and an article does not, you have made an argument about the shape of the world your organisation operates in. Compare that against the taxonomy you inherited, and the mismatch is usually where the trouble started.

Structure precedes vocabulary

Structure comes first because structure determines which distinctions are even available. If notes and essays share one type, no vocabulary can reliably separate them at query time.

A taxonomy applied to the wrong structure does not fail loudly. It degrades, slowly, into a set of labels nobody trusts.
